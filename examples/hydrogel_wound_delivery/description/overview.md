# Overview

Chronic inflammation after excisional wounds delays re-epithelialization and increases scarring risk. We developed a thermosensitive chitosan–β-glycerophosphate hydrogel loaded with dexamethasone sodium phosphate (Dex-SP) to provide a burst–tailored release profile during the first 72 h after injury.

**Primary hypothesis:** Dex-loaded dressings reduce local IL-1β and TNF-α at day 3 without suppressing systemic cortisol below the laboratory reference interval, compared with vehicle hydrogel and standard gauze.

**Study design:** Randomized, blinded evaluator, three-arm parallel trial in Yorkshire pigs (n = 18, six per arm). Full-thickness dorsal wounds (2 × 2 cm) were treated at closure; dressings were changed at 24 and 48 h. Outcomes included wound area by planimetry, histology (H&E, Masson’s trichrome), and cytokine immunoassays on wound-edge biopsies.

The manuscript argues that matching burst release to the early inflammatory window improves mechanistic plausibility while limiting prolonged immunosuppression.
